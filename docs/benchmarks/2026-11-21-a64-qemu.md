# aarch64 benches under QEMU (2026-11-21, emulated)

**Emulated.** `qemu-aarch64` 11.0.3 user mode (TCG) on an i5-7400 under WSL, idle machine.
The Rust harness is built for `aarch64-unknown-linux-musl` (NVOL's NEON paths against memchr
2.8.3 and aho-corasick 1.1.5). TCG translates every NEON instruction to host code, so
these times compare the two implementations' instruction streams under one translator. They
are not hardware speed: on an ARM core, real NEON throughput, latencies and the branch
predictor decide (1.7a, still open for real hardware). The `delta` is positive when NVOL is
faster.

What stands out:
- **`bench`:** memchr, memrchr, memchr2 and memchr3 are on par (−1.4…+2.6 %).
  `count NL (sad)` and `memchr_iter().count()` are 5-6× faster. The memchr side runs at the
  speed of its per-match iteration (0.18-0.60 GB/s, like `count NL (parity)`); the reason
  for its aarch64 path has not been checked in its source yet.
- **`membench`:** on par or better from 4 KiB, and +12…+35 % at 64 B. The exception is
  `Finder::find 3B frequent "the"`, at **−5.0…−7.8 %** in every size. That is a match right
  at the start, so the cost is the call and the prefilter setup, not the scan. Recorded in item 31.
- **`tedbench`:** NEON Teddy is on par or ahead in every case (−0.7…+10.1 %).

Odin on aarch64 (2.1c) is not included: the Odin benches need an `odin` that targets
`linux_arm64`, and none is installed in WSL.
